## Glossary of terms

- **AGC (Automatic Generation Control):** Slow outer control loop (integral action on ACE) that restores frequency to 50/60 Hz and tie-line flows to schedule after a disturbance. (§5.3)
- **AVR (Automatic Voltage Regulator):** Feedback controller on a synchronous generator that adjusts field current to hold terminal voltage at setpoint. (§5.4)
- **Balanced three-phase system:** One in which the three phase voltages/currents have equal magnitude and 120° separation, summing to zero. Per-phase analysis works. (§1.1)
- **B-coefficients (loss coefficients):** Quadratic coefficients B<sub>ij</sub> used in the approximate loss formula P<sub>L</sub> = ΣΣ B<sub>ij</sub> P<sub>i</sub> P<sub>j</sub>. (§4.5)
- **Circuit breaker rating (short-circuit MVA):** √3 V<sub>rated</sub> × I<sub>interrupting</sub>; the breaker must exceed the calculated fault MVA at its location. Momentary duty covers the first asymmetric peak including DC offset; interrupting duty covers current at contact parting (a few cycles in, transient level). (§2.6)
- **Coordination equation:** IC<sub>i</sub> × L<sub>i</sub> = λ, the optimality condition for economic dispatch with losses. (§4.5)
- **Critical clearing angle / time (δ<sub>cr</sub>, t<sub>cr</sub>):** The rotor angle (or time after fault initiation) at which the accelerating area equals the available decelerating area exactly; clearing beyond it → instability. (§3.4)
- **Damper winding (amortisseur):** Short-circuited copper bars in the rotor of a synchronous machine; act like the squirrel-cage of an induction motor and are responsible for subtransient behaviour. (§2.3)
- **DC offset:** Decaying DC component that appears in the first few cycles of fault current in any inductive circuit, caused by the inductor's inability to change current instantaneously; worst case at voltage zero-crossing. (§2.2)
- **Droop (speed regulation, R):** Deliberate proportional characteristic of a governor (ΔP<sub>m</sub> = −(1/R)Δf) that lets multiple generators share load stably in parallel. Typical R = 5%. (§5.2)
- **Economic dispatch:** Choosing P<sub>i</sub> to minimise total fuel cost subject to meeting load (plus losses). (§4 intro)
- **Equal incremental-cost rule (equal-λ rule):** For lossless dispatch, all units not at a limit must operate at the same marginal cost λ. (§4.3)
- **Equal-area criterion:** Geometric method (for OMIB systems) for judging transient stability without integrating the swing equation; accelerating area = decelerating area at the stability limit. (§3.4)
- **Exciter / excitation system:** Separate DC or AC source supplying DC field current to the rotor of a synchronous generator; controls reactive power and terminal voltage. (§5.4)
- **Fault:** Any abnormal connection that interferes with normal current flow — shunt faults (LG, LL, LLG, 3φ) and series faults (open conductors). (§2.1)
- **Flyball governor:** Centrifugal speed sensor at the heart of a mechanical-hydraulic turbine governor. (§5.2)
- **Infinite bus:** A voltage source so large its magnitude and angle are unaffected by anything connected to it; used as the "rest of the system" in one-machine studies. (§3.3)
- **Input-output curve C<sub>i</sub>(P<sub>i</sub>):** Hourly operating cost of generator i as a function of its MW output; usually fit by a quadratic. (§4.2)
- **Incremental fuel cost IC<sub>i</sub> = dC<sub>i</sub>/dP<sub>i</sub>:** The cost of producing one additional MW from plant i; a rising straight line when C is quadratic. (§4.2)
- **Isochronous governor:** Zero-droop governor that holds speed exactly (used on isolated units; unstable when paralleled). (§5.2)
- **Load-frequency control (LFC):** The combined primary (droop) + secondary (AGC) system that regulates frequency and tie-line flows. (§5 intro)
- **Momentary duty:** The first-cycle peak current (including DC offset) that a breaker must survive when closing into a fault. (§2.6)
- **Negative sequence:** A balanced three-phase set in **reverse** phase order (acb); produced by unbalanced operation; motors/generators present Z<sub>2</sub> ≠ Z<sub>1</sub>. (§1.2)
- **OMIB (one-machine infinite-bus):** The canonical stability model — one generator behind X connected to a fixed infinite bus; leads to P<sub>e</sub> = (EV/X) sin δ. (§3.3)
- **Penalty factor L<sub>i</sub> = 1/(1 − ∂P<sub>L</sub>/∂P<sub>i</sub>):** Multiplier on IC<sub>i</sub> accounting for incremental transmission losses when plant i is raised by 1 MW. (§4.5)
- **Per-unit (pu) system:** Normalising voltages, currents, powers, impedances to chosen base values; makes transformers disappear. (§0.3.2)
- **Positive sequence:** The normal balanced three-phase set (abc order), rotating forward; this is the only sequence containing generator EMFs. (§1.2)
- **Power-angle curve:** P<sub>e</sub>(δ) = (EV/X) sin δ, the central curve of Unit III. (§3.3)
- **Sequence decoupling:** For symmetric power-system elements (transposed lines, symmetric transformers, etc.), currents of one sequence produce voltage drops only of that same sequence — so the three sequence networks are independent except at the fault point. (§1.2, §1.10)
- **Sequence networks:** Three separate single-phase equivalent circuits (positive, negative, zero) derived from the original three-phase system; each contains the corresponding-sequence impedance of every element. (§1.13)
- **Stability — steady-state, transient, dynamic:** Respectively, ability to stay in sync under small gradual changes, after a large disturbance, and under small disturbances including control action. (§3.1)
- **Subtransient / transient / steady-state reactances (X″<sub>d</sub>, X′<sub>d</sub>, X<sub>d</sub>):** The effective direct-axis reactance seen during, respectively, the first 1–2 cycles (damper + field + armature-reaction paths), the next few cycles (field path only), and steady state (only X<sub>a</sub> + X<sub>l</sub>). (§2.3)
- **Swing equation:** M d²δ/dt² = P<sub>m</sub> − P<sub>e</sub>; Newton's second law for a generator rotor. (§3.2)
- **Swing curve:** Plot of δ(t) from a point-by-point numerical integration; unbounded monotonic growth means loss of synchronism. (§3.5)
- **Symmetrical components:** Fortescue's 1918 transformation decomposing any three unbalanced phasors into three balanced sets (positive, negative, zero). (§1.2)
- **Thevenin equivalent at the fault:** Each sequence network, viewed from the fault bus F, is replaced by its prefault open-circuit voltage (V<sub>f</sub> for positive, 0 for negative and zero) in series with Z<sub>th</sub>. (§2.4)
- **Transposed transmission line:** A line whose phases are physically rotated so each occupies each position for 1/3 of the length; makes the self and mutual reactances symmetric and so enables sequence decoupling. (§1.10)
- **Unit commitment:** The problem of deciding *which* units to start/shut down over the daily/weekly cycle; separate from (and solved before) economic dispatch. (§4.4)
- **Y-Δ transformer phase shift:** The standard ANSI convention: HV positive-sequence phasors lead LV by +30°; negative lags by −30°; zero sequence experiences no shift but is governed by winding/grounding connections. (§1.9)
- **Zero sequence:** Three in-phase identical phasors; V<sub>a0</sub> = V<sub>b0</sub> = V<sub>c0</sub>, I<sub>n</sub> = 3 I<sub>a0</sub>; flows only when a neutral/ground return path exists. (§1.2, §1.7)
